The wrong question

“Is my English good enough?”

Almost every executive asks me this in our first conversation. The right question is a different one: good enough for what? For which room, with whom on the other side, on what date?

Your English isn't behind. Your idea is.

Communication isn't measured when it leaves you. It's measured when it arrives. If the message isn't clear in your own language, it won't arrive whole in English.

The Student Trap

The more you study like a student, the more you wait to be corrected.

And students don't negotiate, lead or give talks. Reading, taking notes and reviewing feel like progress. Confidence comes from speaking, recording and rehearsing the real situation.

You're not a student. You're a speaker.

Inductive Method

The path has four steps.

Every process starts from your scene, not from grammar. The four steps work together: remove one and the whole result weakens.

  1. 1

    Scene

    Scene Map

    The date, the room, who decides on the other side and what's at stake: the role, the contract, the position.

  2. 2

    Essence

    Command Voice

    The idea organized before it becomes English. The same authority in both languages, without the emotional block.

  3. 3

    Niche

    Insider Vocabulary

    The language of your world: law, medicine, negotiation, trade. What you won't use stays out.

  4. 4

    Action

    Real Rehearsal

    The scene rehearsed until it becomes memory, with short daily practice and feedback. No reading, no note-taking.

Across every step: body, voice and emotion. Posture, diction and presence.

Opening night is never the first time.
